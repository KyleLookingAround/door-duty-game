Reputation

Stars from every job add up. They decide which clients will talk to you (0★ to 14★ on the High Street) and which premises will rent to you (6★ workshop, 14★ HQ).

Stars

★ pass the client's goals. ★★ at or under the surveyor's par cost. ★★★ under par with no bumps or safety stops. More stars, more maintenance money.

The surveyor

Every contract is solved by simulation before it's offered: targets come from the best design found, so every job is provably beatable and never a repeat of your last few answers.

Landmarks

Structure

One town that grows

Everyday buildings evolve

The café, bank and hotel never go stale. New tech brings new twists to them: research queue barriers and the bank asks you to sort its cash-machine queue.

Sites have character

Every building comes with fixed street furniture that shapes the crowd: café tables, a cash-machine queue, a taxi rank, library steps with one ramp, portico columns, a bus stop dropping surges. Reading the site is part of the puzzle.

New clients arrive

As your reputation grows, new buildings appear on the map: an office block, a cinema, a second hotel. Fresh clients without a whole new area.

Landmarks are finales

Locked sites sit on the map showing what they need. Each opens with a hand-built opening night, then generates multi-stage jobs where one bottleneck feeds the next.

More that fits

Other ideas

New guests

  • Umbrellas on rainy days make everyone wider.
  • Prams and delivery trolleys need a step-free door too.
  • Cyclists with bikes can't use a revolving door at all.
  • Tourists ride the door round on purpose to take photos.
  • Phone zombies react late and miss their exit.
  • Kids run ahead and race the wings.

Calendar and weather

  • Heatwave: doors propped open, and cooling replaces heating.
  • Christmas windows stop shoppers dead outside the store.
  • Black Friday opening rush at the department store.
  • Marathon day closes one side of the street.
  • Rush-hour shape: a test that runs a whole day, in by morning and out by evening.

Running the firm

  • Rival firm bids on the same tenders: undercut them on price or beat them on stars.
  • Wear and servicing: neglected doors slow down and jam.
  • Insurance: every bump becomes a claim on your premiums.
  • Surprise inspections from the fire officer or an access auditor.
  • Green star: the client's heating bill as a score.

Long-term hooks

  • Paradox journal: each paradox you master is written up with the real science behind it.
  • Blueprint book: save a design and reuse it on later jobs.
  • Local paper headlines about your best and worst jobs.
  • Daily contract on a shared seed so friends can compare costs.
  • Programmable doors: write rules like "if the queue is over 10, speed up".

  3. Street furniture and new guests

    Every building has its own furniture: café tables, a cash-machine queue, a reception desk, a taxi rank, library steps with one ramp, a bus stop that drops surges, portico columns. People route round it, and it changes which doors work.

  4. Pillars, queue barriers and fire drills

    A new Crowd science research branch. Lane barriers split a door into in and out; a pillar just inside an exit takes the crowd's push so the doorway jams less. Fire-drill jobs sound the alarm after the rush: revolving doors fold flat and the lobby has to empty against the clock.

    Built

  5. Calendar, weather and the street map

    The High Street is now a painted street scene you swipe along, with each client's building drawn in its own style and the doors you fitted on its ground floor. It changes through the year: blossom, full trees, autumn leaves, snow and stars. One event a month, shown three months ahead: January sales, storm season, marathon day, a heatwave where lobbies must stay cool, Black Friday and Christmas windows with crowds standing in front of them. Seasonal tenders pay 25% more margin.

    Whole-day rush shape
    Built

  6. Running the firm

    Callbacks come from changes on the street you can see coming on the calendar (a bus stop moving, a care home opening, a cold snap): the clients a change touches have their own doors re-tested, and only those that fall short call, so building with room to spare pays. Doors wear every month: worn ones pay less maintenance, and broken ones pay nothing until you service them (tap the building). Insurance is loaded for every bump on your record, with the odd injury claim. Inspectors re-test old jobs for safety and step-free access: a pass earns a certificate worth 10% more maintenance, and a fail brings a fine and a callback. A lobby kept snug in the cold earns a green star, worth 15% more.

    Built

  7. Market Square, Office Towers and a rival

    A second street opens at 18★: a museum with grand steps, a cinema that empties at once when the film ends, a gym full of cyclists, and a health centre. At its end, Office Towers is the second landmark: the stack effect pulls cold air in at every door, and the day comes in three waves. It needs Draught lobbies, Wide-drum revolving and Heat pumps. Brassworks Ltd starts bidding on tenders you leave sitting, and their cheap work comes back to you as rescue jobs that pay 30% more.

  8. Station Quarter

    The first combination landmark, at the end of the High Street. It only takes bids from a firm with Ticket gates, Tidal-flow gates and Door staff training, plus 16★. Instead of doors you build a gate line: single-file lanes with paddles. The morning rush pours in, then trains empty up the escalators every few seconds. Auto lanes turn to face the heavier queue, and the goal is that 9 in 10 commuters get through within a set time.

  9. Inside jobs, Riverside and chained levels

    Every remaining paradox, as work inside buildings: Mercer & Sons' counter hall, the escalators at Halden's and the station, the lift lobbies at Atlas House and the towers, the Town Hall paternoster. Riverside opens at 30★ with Skyport (pier walkway and baggage reclaim), the Galleria (Braess's paradox) and Apollo Arena, whose sell-out night chains bag check, escalators and concourse into one crowd: fix one bottleneck and the crush moves to the next. Finished research brings calls from the clients who could use it.
